06 — One rule for today

We are designing it blind.

The brief came from their marketing site — but we have deliberately not looked at the real CostGuard screens. We will build from the data, the design system and first principles — and compare at the end. Everything from here on is the method: skills, research, screen.

09 — The method

Ask an AI for a screen, and you get a screen anyone could have made.

It does not know your components, your grid, your data, or the twelve promises your product has already broken. So it invents all of them — plausibly, and wrongly.

10 — The fix

It isn't a better prompt. It's onboarding.

A skill is the document you would hand a new hire on day one — where the tokens live, which components already exist, what the data can and cannot answer, which arguments are already settled. Written once, read every time.

14 — What both prompts have in common

Seven parameters. Neither had them all.

Skill one forgot its tools and its definition of done. Skill two forgot its triggers — and half its method, the reference. That is what the checklist is for: not a longer prompt, but seeing what is missing before it costs a rebuild. And note what still isn't in either one: no format, no sections, no tone. The structure comes out of the conversation.

This is the part people get backwards. A longer prompt does not make a better skill. A prompt that walks the checklist — and still ends with ask me questions — does.

15 — Skill one

It asked before it built.

  • ?How wide should this be? A skill for this one brief, or a PM for any Finout screen?
  • ?Who does competitor research? Market and features here — or UI patterns, which belong to the next skill?

Two questions, two minutes. Without them we would have built a one-page generator and called it a pipeline.

16 — Skill two

It asked too.

  • ?How literal is “agent”? Expertise baked into the skill, or real subagents fanning out per competitor?
  • ?What lands in Figma? Raw screenshots, or an annotated board with a comparison matrix?
  • ?What shape are the slides? Standalone, or the same deck shell as everything else?

Three questions. Each one would have been a rebuild if we had guessed wrong.

17 — Five questions, in total

Every one would have been a rebuild.

If we had guessed narrow

A one-page generator

A skill that writes this one brief and nothing else. No pipeline, and nothing to take home.

If we had guessed raw images

A mood board

Screenshots with no matrix and no point of view — inspiration, not research.

The questions took about four minutes. Answering them wrong would have cost the session.

23 — Chapter 01 · what we got

Three calls that shaped the screen.

D1

The unit is the recommendation

Not the resource — our data has no instance IDs. Named, not assumed.

D3

“Realized” has a definition

Cost for 14 days after done, against 14 days before. Computable today.

D6

Never claim $0

No baseline means “Not verifiable”. A zero would say the saving failed.

D6 is the one that created a component. A variance column has to print a number — and there is no honest number for something unknowable.

26 — Chapter 02 · what we got

Twelve references. Nobody solved it.

Scored on four dimensions, nothing scored above 3 on regression. Linear collapses it into one activity line — “moved to Done, then back to In Progress” — buried on a detail page. Snowflake lets you reopen a closed case for “1 week, 2 days, 23 hours”. No list view anywhere says this was done, and it is not done any more.

The most useful research finding was a negative one. Positive findings tell you what to copy. The negative one told us where the single new idea should go.

29 — Chapter 03 · what we got

CostGuard, built in Finout's own design system.

26 — The KPI row

$26,802 found. $1,113 kept.

The first version had four tiles and four buttons, and hid the only number that matters. Three statements now, no buttons — and a bar showing that 4% of everything we identified actually reached the bill.

Regression used to be stated four times on one screen. Now it is said three times, and each one does a different job: the card gives the money, the row is pinned and tinted, the badge gives the state.

27 — The gap, built

The evidence is real data.

134 days of real cost from workshop-data/seed.sql. The weekend sawtooth runs all through April and May, then goes flat on 6 June. Nobody has to trust the claim.

28 — Proving it

Four outcomes, and none of them lie.

Realized $812 (−$18) · Realized $301 (+$11) · Verifying — 7 days to go · Not verifiable. The last one is the honest answer when a migration overlapped the window.

29 — And it runs

One rule the interface will not let you skip.

new ──assign──▶ new(owned) ──start──▶ working ──complete──▶ done │ system verifies ▼ regressed ◀──decay── verified │ not verifiable │ verifying

“Start work” is disabled until somebody owns it. The PRD said a recommendation without a named owner defaults to no one. That is now a guard, not a sentence.

Not three filters — three different questions. The FinOps lead asks what is stuck and who owns nothing. Same data, same design system, no new components.

33 — Engineering & Platform

The engineer wants proof, not a price tag.

Filtered to one team, and the evidence comes first. The money is still there — it is just no longer the argument. Engineers are allergic to being handed someone else's cleanup.

34 — Executives

The executive never sees the table.

One number, and one bar: $1,113 banked · $2,075 lost to regression · $11,757 in progress · $12,970 never started. The sliver of green against the rest is the entire programme, in one glance.

35 — What to take from this

Three things.

  • 1Context beats prompting. The skill is not clever wording — it is the onboarding doc that makes every future prompt shorter.
  • 2Make the handoff a contract. Named sections turn separate tools into a chain that survives being handed to someone else.
  • 3Design from what is already promised. Your product is full of buttons that go nowhere. That is a backlog somebody already justified.

36 — And finally

We designed it blind.
Then we checked.

The stages we aimed at were the two they have not built. The users we found in the data were the three they name on their own site. Finout's designer is in the room — so the question is no longer whether we were close. It is what we still got wrong, and that is the most interesting conversation of the day.

37 — Comparison design

One screen.
Three designs.

Same data, same PRD, same design system underneath — and three different answers to what CostGuard should feel like. That is the point of a fast pipeline: a direction costs an hour, not a sprint.

38 — Direction A · the build

Faithful. Ships tomorrow morning.

Tabs + table, straight from Finout's design system. Evidence lives one click away, in the drawer. Optimizes for: fitting the product today.

39 — Direction B · the evidence rail · live

The proof becomes the interface.

What changed: tabs became status groups with money totals, the drawer became an always-on evidence rail — chart, verdict, history, next action. Optimizes for: trust without clicking. Open live →

39 — Direction D · the triage queue · live

One decision at a time.

What changed: the table became a ranked queue — the screen picks what's next and says why now; the program bar shows where every identified dollar stands. Optimizes for: action — clearing the queue. Open live →

40 — Direction C · the concept

The screen talks first.

What changed: the table became an attention stream, every card carries its own sparkline, and the copilot opens the conversation — “Snowflake stopped holding. Reopen for Amit?” Optimizes for: desire — the demo that sells the roadmap.

41 — The difference

Same truth, four temperatures.

A · The build

You browse — evidence one click away

100% existing components. The version you merge this sprint.

B · The evidence rail

You trust — evidence always on screen

One new component. The version you A/B against the drawer.

D · The triage queue

You act — the screen picks what's next

Same components, new posture: a ranked queue with a reason. The version for Monday morning.

C · The concept

It speaks — the copilot opens the conversation

A deliberate break from the system. The version that argues for the vision.

The skill pipeline made all four from the same PRD in one afternoon. Choosing between them is now a product conversation, not a design sprint.
